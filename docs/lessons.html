Field notes · 01

Start with the subject, not the machinery

A technically valid render is not the same thing as a useful film. The argument comes first.

01

Use the material the owner already has.

The kit includes a packaged reader for selected text, slide and document content. The director should start from permitted sources instead of making the owner rewrite a deck as a new brief.Read what is approved. Preserve its references. Ask about what is still missing.

02

Readable text is not verified evidence.

A successful extraction says the text was read, not that its claims are current or that publication is permitted. The brief must distinguish source statements, unresolved questions and proposed creative choices.Evidence limits the claim; an attractive story does not expand the evidence.

03

The brief sets the shape.

Choose the film form, duration and scene structure for the audience and takeaway. The bundled fictional example is optional. It is not the template every new story has to fit.One idea per scene. A conclusion for a title. No demo-shaped assumptions.

Field notes · 02

Listen to the words. Measure the timing.

A voice setting is an input. The actual sound and timing are what the audience receives.

04

Timing follows recorded speech.

The narration tools retain measured timings and word cues. Text length alone cannot prove a scene is paced correctly, and a successful speech request does not prove its pronunciation or delivery sounds right.Measure the speech, then watch and listen to the film.

05

Speech and display text have different jobs.

The kit supports voice, rate and pronunciation substitutions. A spoken substitution should not silently change the product spelling in a diagram or caption. Changing those controls also changes the processing that needs approval.Correct the voice without rewriting what the audience reads.

06

A revision is new work, not permission to erase the old cut.

The local narration-change demonstration regenerated one changed scene and reused unchanged scene audio and word cues. It preserved the previous cut and required new acceptance for the revision. Visual-only revisions were also demonstrated.Reuse what still matches. Preserve what came before. Review the changed cut.

Field notes · 03

What the frame promises

The picture should make the explanation clearer without changing what the evidence means.

07

A font name is not a font.

The local work included a Graphik correction and a shared header/footer treatment across all three film forms. The renderers load and validate local font assets; a declared family name with an unintended fallback is not the same result.Provide authorized HPE Graphik Regular, Medium and Bold. Missing assets should stop the render.

08

Framing should explain, not imply.

This edition uses supplied recordings and approved excerpts, crops and annotations. A zoom is not evidence of an event, and a recording of one screen does not establish an unrecorded handover or outcome. It does not include an automatic product-capture workflow.Show what was recorded. Label what could otherwise be misunderstood.

09

An inspection sample is not complete privacy coverage.

Selected-frame checks and optional local OCR can inform an inspection. They do not prove that every moving frame was reviewed. Keep raw media and raw OCR out of model context; use owner-cleared summaries and make the inspection scope explicit.State what was inspected, and what still needs a human decision.

Two visual systems, two jobs

These guides use Fraunces and DM Mono for an editorial reading experience. The films use HPE Graphik, a dark-green shared header and footer, HPE green accents and reserved caption space. Web typography is not a substitute for the film's authorized font assets.

Color and motion should explain structure and qualifications. The visual treatment does not grant font redistribution rights or broader publication permission.

Field notes · 04

Make the next step honest

Good continuity preserves both completed work and the limits of what is known.

10

A restart needs saved state, not a confident memory.

A fresh director Chat resumed the existing films from saved checkpoints and verified their inputs. The kit checks saved generations and hashes; a changed input is a reason to stop and reconcile, not to reuse an old approval blindly.Ask before saving. Resume from the authorized handoff. Verify before continuing.

11

A reviewer needs evidence, not only a receipt.

In the local workflow, fact and synchronization reviews returned insufficient_evidence. Their input packets did not contain enough of the source or timing material needed to settle the questions. Hashes can bind files, but cannot tell a reviewer what those files show.Supply cleared, relevant evidence. Keep missing evidence visible instead of relabeling it a pass.

12

Setup and acceptance are different finish lines.

The clean-copy setup needed the absolute setup-script path after a later terminal call started in a different directory. The corrected invocation completed and its offline verifier returned ready. That result establishes tool execution, not speech-service access or a finished film.For setup, verify the tools. For delivery, require the owner's review of the exact cut.

A reviewer can identify a problem. A renderer can produce a file. Only the owner can say the film is accepted.
