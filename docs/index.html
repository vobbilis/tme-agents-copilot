Introduction

A film studio you talk to

You know what is worth explaining. You should not have to become a video editor to explain it well.

TME Agents is a filmmaking kit for architects, product managers and technical storytellers. Open its folder in VS Code, select cmo-chat-director, and describe the film you want. The director prepares the inputs and operates the packaged local tools. You do not write scripts, edit JSON or install a custom Copilot plugin.

Your part is familiar: explain the intended audience, approve the story, judge what the pictures show, and watch the result.

You doFor example
Answer a questionWho is watching, and what should they understand afterward?
Approve the wordsConfirm the narration and its evidence before speech processing.
Judge the pictureIs the thing being explained readable, correctly framed and honestly represented?
Watch and give notes“At this scene, shorten the explanation and change that label.”

You also approve the operations that use your files, install software, send narration for speech generation or save work. The director explains those decisions when they matter.

Chapter 02

The filmmaking behind the conversation

The director brings the editorial decisions and the tools together. You should see a story taking shape, not a succession of technical chores.

Story craft

One idea per scene. Titles that say something. Narration grounded in the permitted sources, with creative choices kept distinct from factual claims.

Voice direction

Choose voice and pace. Pronunciation substitutions change spoken text while preserving display and caption spelling. You listen to decide whether it sounds right.

Word-led timing

Measured narration and word cues inform timing. A changed sentence can require new speech, artwork and rendering; the director explains the affected work.

Footage with context

Use approved excerpts and deliberate framing. Keep provenance and limitations visible rather than implying a recording proves more than it shows.

Bounded inspection

Footage tools can inspect selected frames where the optional OCR tooling is available. A sample is a sample, not proof that every frame is safe to share.

Finishing

Produce an MP4 with SRT and VTT captions. Use the local review page, chapters and timestamped notes before exact-cut acceptance and local delivery.

A shared visual frame

All three forms use the shared HPE header and footer treatment, with room for content and captions. HPE Graphik font assets are not included; provide an authorized local copy.

You direct in words. The tools carry out the approved production work.

Chapter 03

Four reviewers. Only you approve.

The director can ask specialists to review cleared text and bounded evidence. Their findings support your judgment; they do not replace it.

SpecialistWhat it contributes
cmo-fact-checkerQuestions unsupported claims, weak sourcing and language that goes beyond the supplied evidence.
cmo-sync-editorExamines supplied timing, word cues and narration bindings for synchronization risks.
cmo-privacy-reviewerReviews cleared privacy summaries and inspection notes, and identifies decisions a human still needs to make. It does not independently inspect raw media.
cmo-producerReviews supplied cut, preservation, acceptance and delivery receipts to propose an evidence-grounded handover.

The specialists have no tools and no approval authority. The director explains the requested review and estimated cost before asking permission. Missing evidence remains a limitation; a reviewer response is not an automatic pass.

Chapter 04

From an idea to a film you accept

Stay in one ordinary Copilot Chat. These are conversational stages, not slash commands you need to learn.

  1. 01 · SET UP

    Open your studio.

    Clone or download the kit, open the whole folder, select the director and approve the required setup. Wait for offline tool verification to report ready.

  2. 02 · BRIEF

    Start with what you have.

    Permit access to the chosen sources. Agree on the audience, takeaway, form and scope. A first film does not need an existing project folder.

  3. 03 · STORY

    Agree on the words and pictures.

    The director proposes a screenplay and visual plan. Correct the story, clarify uncertain claims and decide whether to request specialist review.

  4. 04 · PRODUCE

    Authorize the work.

    Approve the explained speech and local production operations. Supply recordings or font assets when needed. A failed step gets an explanation, not an invented success.

  5. 05 · REVIEW

    Watch, listen and give notes.

    Review the current cut. Request a visual or narration change in ordinary language. The director preserves earlier cuts and asks before additional processing.

  6. 06 · DELIVER

    Accept the exact cut.

    Record your actual watch/listen and privacy-review results. Once the required acceptance is complete, approve delivery of the MP4 and captions to a local folder.

  7. 07 · RESUME

    Return without starting over.

    With permission, save a checkpoint and handoff. A fresh director Chat can verify the saved state and pick up the next unfinished step.

Open the step-by-step prompts for setup, story, production, revisions, delivery and resume.

Chapter 05

Trust comes from clear boundaries

The point is an honest, watchable film. Knowing which work happened locally, which text went to a service and who approved the cut is part of that.

BoundaryWhat it means here
CopilotConversation, approved source text and cleared evidence use your organization's Copilot service. Do not paste raw recordings, screenshots or credentials into Chat.
SpeechEdge TTS receives separately approved narration text. It is an external speech service, not an offline voice engine.
Local workRendering, approved file access and delivery run on your machine. The director asks before processing or saving work.
Fonts and assetsProvide authorized local HPE Graphik fonts and approved source material. The kit does not supply font licenses or grant distribution rights.
Human acceptanceApproval belongs to an exact cut. Opening playback is not proof of watching and listening; an earlier approval cannot silently approve changed media.
